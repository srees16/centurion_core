# Centurion's Golden Target Fails the Evidence

> **Correction, 31 Oct 2026:** REITs and InvITs are not a new source. Centurion closed them on 11 Oct (tracker GT14): Sharpe 0.47 alone, every blend cut CAGR by 2-4 points, and the Sharpe gain was at most +0.03. Read every REIT/InvIT reference below in that light. The owner kept the golden target as the aspiration, so the "realistic target" below is a first milestone.

> **Superseded by measured diagnostics (GT2A, 31 Oct 2026):** stock alpha is +3.6% a year 2008-25 (t 1.38) and +6.0% 2013-25 (t 2.03), not the rough 2.8 below. Stops are not clearly useful (63-session excess -1.07%, t -0.6). Metals gave return, not crash defence: without them the book's worst weeks are almost unchanged. A monthly rebalance added nothing, so turnover alone is not a lever; wider exits may be. Walk-forward DSR is 0.42 at N 61 independent trials, far below the recorded backtest figure.

No: Centurion cannot be expected to reach Sharpe 1.4, 35% CAGR and Calmar 1 consistently as a swing and positional Indian book, and the evidence says the Sharpe and CAGR legs are unreachable under these constraints. Today's live-like 2008-25 record is 13.0% CAGR, Sharpe 0.60 (excess of 6.5%), Calmar 0.44, and those figures already include the D2 haircut, so nothing below charges it again. The sharpest reference point is passive: gold alone earned 13.8% with a better Calmar (0.51), a 60/40 NIFTY and gold mix earned 12.4%, and Centurion's real edge is about +0.12 to +0.17 of Sharpe, not return. Measured regime returns show that perfect foresight of every bear market on NIFTY would still compound only about 24-25%, and a true Sharpe of 0.6 tops out near 24.5% at any leverage. No documented positional system clears the joint target; the sourced long-run ceiling is about 0.8, and the best verifiable Indian rule-based long-run record is about 18.8%, mostly back-filled and before costs. The honest target is Sharpe about 0.8 and 17-18% CAGR at about 15% volatility, with 0.9-1.0 and 21-24% as a stretch. Reaching it depends less on new streams than on repairing a decaying stock signal, cutting turnover that buys almost nothing, and measuring alpha against a passive mix that includes gold.

All Centurion figures come from its own [measured audit](research_notes/Centurion%20golden%20target%20feasibility/centurion_audit.md) of 31 Oct 2026, which has no external URL. Arithmetic labelled "my calculation" is derived here from the audit or cited sources. It uses Sharpe excess of 6.5% and CAGR ≈ 6.5% + Sharpe × vol − vol²/2.

## Centurion beats a passive mix on risk, not return

The right opening comparison is not NIFTY, which any book holding gold beats easily over a window that starts at the January 2008 peak. It is the mix a patient investor could hold with no engineering at all. This table is one recorded look over 2008-25, monthly rebalanced, and the audit labels it "not a trial".

| Series, 2008-25 | CAGR | Sharpe | Vol | MaxDD | Calmar |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Golden target** | **35%** | **1.4** | **about 22% implied** | **-35% at most** | **1.0** |
| Centurion 75 + gold 12.5 + gilts 12.5 | 12.6% | 0.65 | 9.2% | -20.7% | 0.61 |
| **Centurion deployed, live-like** | **13.0%** | **0.60** | **11.0%** | **-29.5%** | **0.44** |
| Gold (GOLDBEES) alone | 13.8% | 0.52 | 15.1% | -27.3% | 0.51 |
| Passive 60 NIFTY / 40 gold | 12.4% | 0.48 | 13.1% | -39.2% | 0.32 |
| Passive 50 NIFTY / 25 gold / 25 gilts | 10.9% | 0.43 | 10.6% | -32.2% | 0.34 |
| NIFTY total return | 9.8% | 0.25 | 20.4% | -59.4% | 0.17 |
| MCX-B stream | 9.7% | 0.25 | 18.8% | -41.1% | 0.24 |
| Gilts proxy | 6.5% | 0.01 | 5.3% | -12.8% | 0.51 |

Three things follow. **Centurion's CAGR edge over passive is only +0.6 points against 60/40 and +2.1 against 50/25/25, and it trails gold alone by 0.8.** Its Sharpe edge of +0.12 to +0.17 is real in the record but small against a standard error of about 0.24-0.26 on one 18-year Sharpe. The standard error of the difference depends on a correlation the audit does not give, so significance is unmeasured. The edge also arrived late: Centurion against 50/25/25 ran 3.7% v 9.0% in 2008-12, 13.6% v 9.3% in 2013-19 and 21.5% v 15.0% in 2020-25. And gold's 2008-25 run flatters every line that holds it, including Centurion, whose metals were chosen knowing gold and silver survived and rose.

The passive table also closes a lead from the research. A third-party 50/25/25 backtest over Nov 2016-Dec 2025 showed 13.2% at 8.8% vol, about 0.82 Sharpe, and looked better than Centurion's sleeve books ([Multibagg](https://www.multibagg.ai/market-pulse/articles/nifty-gold-allocation-backtests-india-cmoxatc420007qm0jcqu7c76s)). Over 2008-25 the same mix is 0.43. That 0.82 was a gold-decade artefact.

Scored against the target, only one leg passes, and that pass is qualified. The required move is 2.3x the Sharpe, 2x the volatility and 2.7x the CAGR at the same time. Even a Sharpe of 1.4 at today's 11% vol gives only about 21% (my calculation).

| Leg | Target | 2008-25 live-like | 2013-25 live-like | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sharpe | ≥ 1.4 | 0.60 | 0.88 | Unreachable as a sustained live result |
| CAGR | ≥ 35% | 13.0% | 16.9% | Unreachable without ruinous leverage |
| Calmar | ≥ 1 | 0.44 | 0.81 | Reachable in calm windows; not across a 2008-type tail on current evidence |
| PBO | ≤ 30% | 29.2% on excess, 51.2% raw | n/a | Passes only on excess returns |

Reading the ladder needs care. The fall from in-sample 1.16 to 0.60 is often quoted as decay, but it mixes windows. On the same 2013-25 window it is 1.16 to 0.88, or 24%, and the rest comes from adding 2008-12. The -29.5% drawdown comes from the 2008 tail, which is why simple volatility arithmetic misjudges it: at 12% vol a Gaussian model puts the expected wait for that loss near 230 years (my reading of the elite-systems notes), and it happened once in 18. **Every Gaussian drawdown odd in this report is therefore a floor, not an estimate.**

## Every system that clears this bar runs a different business

Only one record clearly exceeds these numbers, and it is not a precedent. Medallion reported a mean 66.1% gross and 39.2% net over 1988-2018 with no negative gross year ([Cornell, excerpt](https://www.scribd.com/document/470291821/SSRN-id3504766-pdf)). None of that is publicly audited, and Guo and Liu argue its compounded pre-fee return was "probably under 35%" ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.10917)). Its mechanics are reported as a 50.75% trade accuracy ([Institutional Investor](https://institutionalinvestor.com/article/b1k2fymby99nj0/Famed-Medallion-Fund-Stretches-Explanation-to-the-Limit-Professor-Claims)), "ample and cheap" leverage ([The7Circles](https://the7circles.uk/?p=35029)), and a size limit that keeps it near $10bn. Holds of a day or two come only through secondary, estimate-labelled text ([Faster Than Normal](https://fasterthannormal.co/businesses/renaissance-technologies)).

**What characterises the winners is breadth, cheap financing and capped capacity, not a better signal.** At 50.75%, Sharpe 1.4 needs about 8,700 independent bets a year. Centurion's 52% buy hit rate, under the same symmetric-payoff simplification, needs about 1,220 (my calculation), and it makes 420 trades a year across a median 20 correlated names. Renaissance's own public funds are the natural experiment. RIEF made +8.5% and RIDA +3.2% against Medallion's +76% in 2018 ([Institutional Investor](https://institutionalinvestor.com/article/b1k2fymby99nj0/Famed-Medallion-Fund-Stretches-Explanation-to-the-Limit-Professor-Claims)), and RIDA lost 31.6% in 2020 ([Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2e0uykr3vn5booz0smrcw/hedge-funds/renaissances-2024-rebirth)). Linking that gap to horizon is inference, because RIEF's holding period is undocumented.

Positional live records cluster far lower. Berkshire's 0.76-0.79 is the best long-run Sharpe among stocks and funds with 30+ year histories, at about 1.6x cheap leverage ([SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=3197185); [AQR](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Research/Journal-Article/Buffetts-Alpha)). Man AHL Diversified shows 9.86% at 0.44 over 30 years ([factsheet excerpt](https://swissfunddata.ch/sfdpub/docs/fsm-2320_00_01-20260828-en.pdf)). SG Trend shows 0.47 through Jun 2026 in one paper ([Sepp and Lucic](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.19497v1)) and 0.30 through Feb 2024 in another ([Invesco](https://invesco.com/content/dam/invesco/emea/en/pdf/RRE_2024_Q2_NavigatingMomentum.pdf)). Those two cannot both be on one basis, since the longer window includes a further 20% loss, so read it as 0.3-0.5. Higher CAGRs came with ruin-sized drawdowns: Dunn about 12% at -60% ([AutumnGold](https://autumngold.com/sweet/cta_profile.php?id=393)), Mulvaney about 18% at -61% ([AutumnGold](https://www.autumngold.com/Advisor/cta_profile.php?id=341)). The nearest thing to a 30% multi-decade positional record is Druckenmiller's Duquesne, loosely reported, discretionary, with no Sharpe ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanley_Druckenmiller)). Carver, the only practitioner source with usable numbers, puts the absolute expected maximum at 1.0 "regardless of how good the backtest is" ([notes on p. 146](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1234306875379683328.html)). Centurion's 0.60 already sits inside his 0.40-0.80 band for dynamic systems ([summary](https://the7circles.uk/?p=23464)).

India is no kinder, and no figure below is audited. The longest quant PMS found, Capitalmind Adaptive Momentum, shows about 17% since March 2019, but its trailing five-year figure went from 30.2% in June 2024 ([Capitalmind](https://capitalmind.in/blog/capitalmind-pms-surpasses-2000-crore-in-aum-demonstrating-exceptional-market-performance)) to 7.1% in October 2026 ([Capitalmind](https://www.capitalmind.in/portfolio-management-service/momentum)). Nifty200 Momentum 30 TRI shows 18.8% since April 2005, before costs, with only about six live years, and returned 35% or more in 8 of 21 financial years while still compounding at 18.8% ([HDFC MF deck](https://files.hdfcfund.com/s3fs-public/Others/2026-07/HDFC%20NIFTY200%20Momentum%2030%20Index%20Fund_PPT%20%28July%202026%29.pdf)). Single-year precedent for 35% is common. Sustained precedent is absent. The seven-year Category III top-quartile threshold was 14.48% across only 15 schemes ([CRISIL](https://www.dspim.com/media/pages/documents/31db646381-1757771758/benchmark-report_dsp-india-enhanced-equity-fund_cat-iii_sep-2024.pdf)). The highest 10-year PMS figure found is Aequitas at 26.43%, with internally inconsistent data ([AltportFunds](https://www.altportfunds.com/evaluating-the-best-pms-in-india-2025-rankings-fees-performance-comparisons/)). "No precedent" here means none in sources reachable by this research, and several long-record Indian investors went unsearched. Those names could change the discretionary rows, not the rule-based ones.

Backtests cannot bridge the gap either. Bank alternative-beta products fell from a median 1.20 in backtest to 0.31 live, and only 18 of 215 matched their backtest ([CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/big-ideas/live-performance-of-alternative-beta-products/)). Published anomalies lose roughly a quarter out of sample and 56-58% after publication ([McLean and Pontiff copy](https://Www.Gwern.net/doc/economics/2016-mclean.pdf); [Falck et al](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2105.01380)). An honest backtest would need to show about 2.8 to 5.2 to expect 1.4 live, a level Carver treats as overfitting ([summary](https://the7circles.uk/?p=23464)).

## Where the evidence says the target is unreachable

**The market itself does not contain 35% unlevered.** The audit's regime table reconstructs correctly when weighted by days in log terms, giving 13.1% for the book against 13.0% actual and 9.7% for NIFTY against 9.8% (my calculation). That makes it usable for what-ifs.

| What-if, regime shares 49 / 34 / 17% | Bull | Range | Bear | CAGR |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Book as measured | 30.1% | 7.0% | -15.4% | 13.1% |
| Bull capture lifted to 1.0 | 42.0% | 7.0% | -15.4% | 18.1% |
| Range capture lifted to 1.0 | 30.1% | 13.6% | -15.4% | 15.5% |
| Bears flat, earning nothing | 30.1% | 7.0% | 0% | 16.4% |
| NIFTY with every bear skipped, earning cash | 42.0% | 13.6% | 6.2% | 25.3% |
| **Bull return required for 35%, others as measured** | **86.5%** | 7.0% | -15.4% | 35.0% |

These regimes are ex-post, so every line is an upper bound on what timing can achieve. **Holding NIFTY with perfect foreknowledge of every bear market gives about 25%.** Reaching 35% with range and bear unchanged needs 86.5% a year in bulls, a capture of 2.06 against today's 0.73, and that from a book with beta 0.17. The audit's own bootstrap agrees: five-year CAGR runs p10 4.3%, median 13.2%, p90 23.2%, so even the 90th percentile misses by 12 points.

**Leverage cannot manufacture Sharpe, and at 0.6 it cannot reach 35% at all.** Growth peaks near 24.5% at full Kelly and 60% vol (my calculation from the elite-systems notes). A Sharpe of 0.9 needs about 41% vol, with roughly an 85% chance of a -50% drawdown in 18 years. Sharpe 1.2 needs about 27% vol and about an 89% chance of -30%. Even a true 1.4 at 22% vol has about a 43% chance of a -30% drawdown and 17% of -35%, so the Calmar leg can fail for a genuinely elite system.

Centurion's own experiment settles the practical question: levered book on real stock futures 13.9% / 0.45 / -40.3% against 13.2% / 0.62, with 4.2 points a year of futures costs and lowest NAV at 63% of start, 56% without the drawdown rule. That is +0.7 points of CAGR for 27% less Sharpe. Plain 1.75x on NIFTY's -59.9% loses 104.7% held and 81.6% reset daily. At the audited 11% vol, half-Kelly on a 25th-percentile Sharpe of 0.44 gives only 0.26x at 4.2 points of drag, and 1.38x even if drag falls to 1.5 points, or 0.70x at the 10th percentile (my calculation). Broker margin is worse: Zerodha MTF at 0.04% a day ([Zerodha](https://zerodha.com/charges)) is about 14.6% a year, which takes a 13% stream below its unlevered return. **At today's Sharpe the answer is no structural leverage.**

**Diversification alone cannot do it either, and correlation is no longer the main reason.** Sharpe 1.4 from 0.5-0.6 streams needs 6-8 streams at zero correlation, and becomes impossible above average correlation of about 0.13-0.18. Centurion's measured correlations are better than the research feared, so the binding constraint is stream quality. Gilts earn 0.01 and MCX-B 0.25, so book plus MCX is about 0.63 by arithmetic. From there about 1.54 of squared Sharpe is still missing, which is about 17 uncorrelated streams at 0.30 or about 6 at 0.50 (my calculation), in a market with about 4-5 independent risk sources.

My own probability, using the research's prior width of 0.3 around a mean of 0.6, is about 0.4% that the true Sharpe is 1.4 or above today, and about 2% with a mean of 0.8 if the whole rebuild works. Those are judgements, not measurements. **Rolling three-year windows will touch the target in good regimes**, as QSPIX's roughly 20.7% a year over 2021-25 inside about 6.1% over 2018-25 shows ([stock data via search](https://stockanalysis.com/quote/mutf/QSPIX)), and Mulvaney's 45.7% for four years followed about 3.3% for a decade ([Price Action Lab](https://priceactionlab.substack.com/p/the-amazing-performance-of-mulvaney)). Those windows are not consistency.

## The audit shows strong defence, weak offence and a fading signal

**Regimes.** Defence is the book's best feature. It captured 0.23 of bear losses (-15.4% a year against -51.3%) and held 2008 Aug-Oct to -3%, 2010-11 to -1% and 2020 to -6%. In NIFTY's 30 worst weeks it averaged -0.99% against -7.06%. Its one real failure is 2008 H1 at -24%, which sets the -29.5% drawdown, with 2015-16 at -11% next. Offence is weaker: 0.73 bull capture. The neglected state is range, 34% of days, where the book made 7.0% against a 6.2% liquid rate, so a third of the time earns about cash while taking risk.

**Alpha.** The headline is 5.7% a year (t 2.0) against NIFTY, by period -2.3%, +4.4% and +11.2%. It overstates stock skill, because NIFTY is the wrong single benchmark for a book holding 29% metals, and metal P&L correlates -0.01 with stock P&L, so it lands almost entirely in alpha. Metals earned 4.7 points on 29% of capital, about 2.9 points above cash on that capital. **That leaves stock-selection alpha near 2.8 points a year**, roughly half the headline and unlikely to be significant alone (rough calculation from simple-sum attribution). Ex-metals alpha looks close to the +5 point range in both later periods and strongly negative in 2008-12.

**Signal quality.** Rank IC rises with horizon: 0.037 at 5 days, 0.051 at 21, 0.079 at 63, with ICIR 0.85 at 21 and 1.38 at 63, so the signal is slower than the rebalance. The 21-day IC ran 0.060, 0.070, then 0.021 in 2020-25. **IC and alpha move in opposite directions by period**: 2020-25 has the worst IC and the best alpha, and 2008-12 a good IC and negative alpha. So recent alpha cannot be credited to the forecast. Metals, regime tailwind or concentration at the top of the ranking are the candidates, and this is the first thing to investigate. The alpha t of 2.0 over 18 years implies an information ratio near 0.47, which fits a transfer coefficient of about 0.34-0.55 on those ICIRs, within the 0.3-0.8 range for constrained managers ([Clarke et al](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/financial-analysts-journal/2002/portfolio-constraints-and-the-fundamental-law-of-active-management)). On the Fundamental Law, then, a perfect transfer of the best ICIR, costless and unconstrained, would just about equal 1.4. That is a ceiling on the signal, not a forecast (my inference).

**Buys and sells.** Next-21-session excess after each decision is +1.50% for buys (hit 52%), +0.97% for rank exits, +1.39% for rebalance sells, +0.25% for regime exits and -1.24% for stops. **Only stops clearly remove losers.** A rebalance swap earns 0.11% gross over 21 sessions (1.50 less 1.39), below the 0.22% statutory delivery round trip ([Zerodha rates](https://zerodha.com/charges)) before slippage. That swap is value-destroying on this horizon, though a longer-horizon check is needed before removal. Turnover of 420 trades a year costs about 2.2 points, or 0.20 Sharpe at 11% vol.

**Risk management.** A long-only unlevered book with an 8% name cap has almost none of the failure modes behind LTCM, Archegos, XIV or option-seller blow-ups, which combine leverage or short convexity with an exit that is not there ([GAO on LTCM](https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/web/20081126185007/http://www.gao.gov/archive/2000/gg00003.pdf); [Khandani-Lo](https://web.mit.edu/~alo/www/Papers/august07.html)). A new measured weakness is concentration of defence. Book-NIFTY correlation falls from 0.52 calm to 0.19 in crashes while book-gold rises from 0.21 to 0.55: **in crashes the book becomes largely a gold position.** That protection rests on one asset that has just returned 38.4% over three years ([Multibagg](https://www.multibagg.ai/market-pulse/articles/nifty-gold-allocation-backtests-india-cmoxatc420007qm0jcqu7c76s)) and is now correcting ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/markets/stocks/story/gold-price-selloff-jefferies-calls-mcx-shares-a-hedge-against-crude-volatility-heres-why-563543-2026-10-26)).

**Survivorship.** The core is clean: 1,038 universe names, 291 gone and kept in the data, and a survivors-only run adds just 0.10 CAGR points. The live bias sits one level up, in instrument choice, and metals are its largest instance. Stock-level rigour is therefore not the problem. Asset-level hindsight is.

**A record conflict.** The audit's header says the 2026 holdout is unread, while its statistics section says it was opened once on 15 Sep, showing 26.9% annualised and Sharpe 1.04. This report treats it as opened once, hence spent as a clean test. With a Sharpe standard error near 1.1-1.2 on under a year of data, it cannot distinguish 0.6 from 1.4 or from zero. The 30-session paper result (-0.71% against NIFTY -0.93%) is equally uninformative.

Two evidence notes sit behind the bottom rows. A permanent put programme costing about 8% a year would exceed Centurion's whole excess return, and divesting beat protective puts on drawdown ([Harvey et al summary](https://the7circles.uk/?p=28982); [Israelov](https://alphaarchitect.com/pathetic-protection/)). Indian evidence gives no long-side earnings drift ([Gupta and Dhusia](https://journals.vutbr.cz/index.php/trends/article/view/541)), inclusions reverse within 60 days ([Chhatwani](https://publishingindia.com/archive/jcar/price-and-volume-effects-of-nifty-50-index-reorganization)), and size pays little per unit of risk at Sharpe 0.47-0.51 with -76% to -82% drawdowns ([nifty-factor-analytics](https://github.com/RohitVarre/nifty-factor-analytics)).

The factor leads need the same caution. The index table runs April 2005-October 2026, gross, mostly back-filled, on a 6% risk-free rate, so its Sharpes are not comparable with Centurion's 2008-25 net figures. Chain-linking the HDFC deck's financial years from FY09 to FY26 gives about 15.2% for Momentum 30 and 10.3% for Nifty 50 (my calculation), and momentum's live excess is much thinner than its back-fill: 0.8 points over five years against 4.9 since base. Centurion's Calmar of 0.44 still beats every index in that table (0.35 at best).

## Few genuinely new streams exist, and most candidates are already held

The research ranked gold first among diversifiers, but it was written without sight of Centurion: gold is already 29% of capital alongside silver, and supplies much of the 0.60. Its illustrative 1.06 book Sharpe counted gold twice. Replacing its estimates with measured streams changes the picture.

| Stream | Stand-alone Sharpe | Book corr calm / crash | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gold | 0.52 measured, flattered | 0.21 / 0.55 | Already held; not new |
| MCX trend | 0.25 measured (MCX-B) | 0.10 / 0.03 | Tested; genuine separation, thin return |
| Gilts | 0.01 measured | 0.07 / 0.04 | Risk reducer only; rates now rising |
| Defined-risk monthly NIFTY options | 0-0.4 estimate | 0.3-0.5 / 0.7+ estimate | A1 already tested at 0.62; capped overlay |
| REIT/InvIT | 0.4-0.6 forward estimate | 0.15-0.34 to Nifty / 0.4-0.6 estimate | **New; best candidate** |
| Stock-futures long-short | 0-0.5 estimate | 0.2-0.5 / 0.4-0.6 estimate | **New; most valuable in-house test** |
| Buybacks, open offers, delisting | Unmeasured | Low / 0.3-0.6 estimate | New but too small to matter |

**REIT/InvIT is the only sizeable new source.** The new 90:10 index shows 12.72% over five years with one-year Nifty correlation 0.15 ([NSE factsheet](https://www.niftyindices.com/Factsheet/Factsheet_Nifty_REITs_and_InvITs_9010.pdf)), and vol of about 9% ([indexPe](https://indexpe.in/nifty-reits-invits-90-10)). That window saw rate cuts while the RBI has now hiked to 5.50% ([5paisa](https://www.5paisa.com/index.php/blog/rbi-mpc-meeting-schedule)), so forward Sharpe of 0.4-0.6 is an estimate, and it shares rate risk with gilts. Its history starts in April 2021 ([JM Financial](https://www.jmfinancialservices.in/market-news-and-insights/1713371)), so **it cannot pass a 2008-25 gate**. It needs an explicit, recorded exception at a small risk weight, or it stays out.

**A stock-futures short leg is the other real unknown.** Indian long-short funds behave like about 0.4 beta equity on two monthly data points ([PMS Bazaar Jan](https://pmsbazaar.com/Blogs/Jan-2025-AIF-Performance-Market-Pressures-Weigh-on-Returns-Long-Short-Funds-Outshine); [Mar](https://pmsbazaar.com/Blogs/AIFs-Delivered-a-Strong-Comeback-in-March-2025-as-Markets-Rebound)). Pairs results run from "up to 34%" on 2011-17 stock futures ([Aggarwal and Aggarwal](https://ideas.repec.org/a/kap/apfinm/v28y2021i1d10.1007_s10690-020-09317-1.html)) to Sharpe 0.09 on a 2016-25 walk-forward ([QuantInsti](https://blog.quantinsti.com/cointegrated-pairs-trading-indian-equity-market-epat-project/)). Futures STT has since risen to 0.05% ([ICICI Direct](https://www.icicidirect.com/futures-and-options/articles/stt-changes-in-budget-2026-what-f-o-traders-need-to-know)). Judge a short leg on crash behaviour, not stand-alone Sharpe.

**Options are a capped overlay, not an engine.** The fullest cost-aware study finds every systematic short-vol variant net negative over 2015-25, with tail loss the main cause ([Pillai](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6876580), abstract only). SEBI's FY26 study shows 87.7% of individual F&O traders losing ([PolicyEdge](https://www.policyedge.in/p/nearly-9-in-10-individual-fo-traders-lost-money-as-participation-fell-18-sebi-studies)). Excluded on evidence or scope: USD-INR after RBI's 10 Oct 2026 tightening ([TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/?p=1088521)), premium global ETFs at up to 111.8% above NAV ([DSIJ](https://insights.dsij.in/dsijarticledetail/6-global-etfs-in-india-trade-at-premiums-of-up-to-1118-to-nav-60143)), and dispersion, which is short correlation and loses in stress ([Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/strategies/dispersion-trading/)).

The measured crash matrix corrects the research's pessimism. It planned for about 2-3 effective streams in a crash. Centurion's data show equity, gold, rates and MCX trend stayed separate, with NIFTY-MCX -0.18, and in NIFTY's 30 worst weeks gold averaged +0.70%, gilts -0.25% and MCX +1.32%. So Centurion does have about four separated risk sources, but they earn too little. An estimated full set (book 0.60, REIT 0.40, MCX 0.25, gilts 0.01, options 0.20, long-short 0.25) reaches 0.83 at zero correlation, 0.74 at 0.10 and 0.70 at 0.20 with perfectly known inputs (my calculation). **The same set with a stock core repaired to 0.75 gives 0.82-0.94, and with metals deflated so the core is 0.50 it gives 0.63-0.76.** The core's Sharpe enters squared and outweighs every candidate stream.

The Centurion 75 / gold 12.5 / gilts 12.5 line is the best row in the passive table at 0.65 and Calmar 0.61, but it lifts metals to about 34% of capital at a moment of hindsight, so adopting it from this look would be a selection, not a finding. Sleeves left Sharpe flat at 0.61 while halving drawdown to -14.5% because a stream adds only if its Sharpe beats correlation × book Sharpe, and its gain is quadratic.

**Multi-timeframe diversification means going slower, not faster.** Across 84 futures, median Sharpe moved only from 0.55 at a 5-day span to 0.33 at two years, while the square-root law implied about 10x ([Sepp and Lucic](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.19497v1)). Carver's fast crossover has a law-implied 1.68 and an actual 0.07 ([qoppac](https://qoppac.blogspot.com/2020/04/how-fast-should-we-trade.html)). Centurion's own IC ladder says the same, so no swing cadence closes a 2.3x Sharpe gap. Timeframes on the same assets add robustness, not breadth, because they share a driver. The useful version is an equal-weighted ensemble of lookbacks across a plateau, centred on 63 days and slower, with continuous signals, which cut turnover by more than a third without significant loss in Baltas-Kosowski ([SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2140091)). Holding all variants also removes the best-of-N selection premium, so the number reported is closer to honest.

## Lean into uptrends with deployment, not borrowing

**The audit undercuts the obvious design.** A causal bull state (NIFTY above a rising 200-day mean) holds 75% of the book's log return, yet NIFTY earned only 5.2% a year in that state against 19.6% in range and 16.9% in bear, because crashes start above the mean and rebounds start below it. Levering index beta on this signal would add exposure in the market's worst forward state. Whatever earns the money there is stock selection and deployment, and part of the 75% simply reflects the book being risk-on then. That rules out the US-style result of 2x above the 200-day, 0.51 against 0.30 ([CXO](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/volatility-effects/leveraging-the-u-s-stock-market-based-on-sma-rules/)), which assumed 1% financing and ignored switching costs.

The defensible sequence has five parts, each pre-registered with a single stated hypothesis and logged as a trial. First, spend idle capital before any borrowing: 30% of capital sits idle on average, and a continuous exposure tilt between about 60% and 100% with hysteresis bands fits the evidence for "sinning a little" ([AQR](https://www.Aqr.Com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Insights/White-Papers/Market-Timing-Sin-a-Little.pdf)) and the whipsaw record of binary rules ([Systematic Investor](https://systematicinvestor.github.io/strategy/Strategy-TAA-BANDS)). The research suggested a 60-120% range; at today's Sharpe and 4.2 points of drag the upper bound should stay at 100%.

Second, let volatility cut exposure but never raise it. Harvey et al find Sharpe gains in equities and credit ([abstract](https://sf.cufe.edu.cn/info/1198/5688.htm)), but 103 equity strategies show no reliable real-time edge ([Cederburg et al](https://www.lehigh.edu/~xuy219/research/COWY.pdf)), and the look-ahead critique lands hardest on the levered side ([Liu et al](https://profiles.wustl.edu/en/publications/volatility-managed-portfolio-does-it-really-work/)). Barroso's near-doubling of momentum Sharpe is long-short, so a long-only book should expect far less ([UCP](https://ciencia.ucp.pt/en/publications/momentum-has-its-moments/)).

Third, keep any regime model to two states with a delay. Out-of-sample work shows vol down about 41% but only 18.5-201.6 bps of excess return ([Bulla et al](https://mpra.ub.uni-muenchen.de/21154/1/MPRA_paper_21154.pdf)), and small misclassification erases the advantage ([Dacco-Satchell](https://iaorifors.com/paper/30956)). Indian factor timing is similarly weak, with only 12-month momentum significant ([Raju 2025](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5513940)). Budget 0-2 points of return at most, and judge the model on false-signal cost.

Fourth, simplify the exits. The book has an ATR stop, a regime exit and a 20/30/35% drawdown ladder. Stops have clear measured value, regime exits sit near neutral at +0.25%, and stop rules add value only under momentum or regime persistence ([Kaminski-Lo](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/114876)). Test each by removal, keep the ladder as ruin insurance, and add no fourth index overlay. Carver's simulations warn against equity-curve filters ([qoppac](https://qoppac.blogspot.com/2015/11/random-data-evaluating-trading-equity.html)).

Fifth, define re-entry before it is needed, because the Daniel-Moskowitz panic state ([NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20439)) reaches a long-only book as lag in the rebound, and because selling the low without a return rule is how the August 2007 reversal was missed ([Khandani-Lo](https://web.mit.edu/~alo/www/Papers/august07.html)). If Sharpe is later confirmed live near 0.9 and drag falls below 2 points, a ceiling of about 1.25-1.5x on index futures becomes defensible, held with liquid collateral of at least 3x posted margin, sized for a -13% no-exit day, and capped by the 10-15% per-cycle loss rule.

## Staying robust means counting honestly and treating live as the only test

The statistics support a modest edge and nothing more. **0.60 is the point estimate, and D2 is not charged again here.** Selection is a separate matter from implementation: 18 years at 0.60 equals the expected best of about 100 skill-less independent trials (my reading of the overfitting notes' calculation), which assumes a pure selection, so read it as a warning rather than a verdict. A further selection and decay discount of 20-35% would give 0.39-0.48. Treat this as the downside case, not the base case, because walk-forward already removes much parameter selection, and the research itself split on whether it applies. The 95% interval on 0.60 runs about 0.1 to 1.1 and cannot be narrowed by more analysis of the same data.

DSR of 0.939 at N 61 misses 0.95 by less than trial-count error can resolve, and at N 7,000 it falls to about 0.83 or 0.48 depending on which Sharpe entered it (hand calculations). N 61 from the research brief's roughly 7,000 runs implies average pairwise correlation near 0.99, plausible within a sweep and not across families, on a formula recalled from memory. **Report DSR at low, central and high N, not a point.** PBO must match the metric actually used to select: if settings were picked on raw returns, 51.2% is the relevant figure and the only passing leg fails. Fix the trial set before computing PBO, because junk trials can flatter it, the same dilution that weakens White's Reality Check ([Hsu et al](https://repository.hku.hk/handle/10722/141768)).

The toolkit that follows is cheap. Keep walk-forward for the live-like number, and add CPCV as a research filter, which beat walk-forward on false discoveries in synthetic tests ([Arian et al](https://www.scribd.com/document/725401650/SSRN-id4778909), excerpt). Use registry-level FDR, which a private registry can do better than academia because it sees its failures ([Chen](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.10275v4)). Add implicit trials such as universes, cost models and features picked from prior knowledge. Use fragility tests as vetoes, since drop-data sensitivity is the retrieved predictor of decay (R² 0.14) that actually works ([Falck et al](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2105.01380)). Prefer simple rules, since the most complex products took a far larger live haircut ([Bailey on Suhonen](https://mathinvestor.org/2017/08/backtest-overfitting-in-smart-beta-investments/)), and use equal-risk weights, since no optimiser reliably beats 1/N with this little data ([DeMiguel et al](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v22y2009i5p1915-1953.html)). ML should enter only as a challenger stream tested for incremental alpha after momentum, given the dispute over complexity results ([Nagel](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:nbr:nberwo:34104)).

**Paper trading cannot validate edge.** Sixty sessions has about 8% power to separate Sharpe 1.0 from 0.5, and that separation takes about 25 years; 0.6 against 0.3 takes about 69 (hand calculations using [Lo's standard error](https://traders.studentorg.berkeley.edu/papers/The-Statistics-of-Sharpe-Ratios.pdf)). Keep the 60-session and five-dry-run gates, relabelled operational readiness, and add the stage the chain lacks: canary capital, the first test that measures fills. The research's ramp, which is opinion without published evidence, runs 5-10% for 3-6 months, then 25%, 50% and full over about two years. It advances on slippage, volatility ratio, turnover and reconciliation, not P&L, because volatility (R² 0.67) and drawdown (0.34) persist while return metrics (0.01-0.02) do not ([CXO on Wiecki](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/big-ideas/in-sample-vs-out-of-sample-performance-of-888-trading-strategies)). Scale down on a CUSUM alarm, not a short loss: a fall from 0.6 to 0 takes roughly 28 months to detect, scaled from [Philips et al](https://rdrr.io/github/chindhanai/GSoC2017/src/R/cusumActMgr.R). Do not tweak on live results ([Arnott et al summary](https://hedgefundalpha.com/news/a-protocol-to-prevent-quants-gone-wild/)).

**Live safety is further along than the research assumed.** A nightly GTT reconcile with missing_stop reporting already exists, closing what the research called its most actionable risk: a GTT fires once, may not fill on a gap, and is cancelled at session end ([Zerodha terms](https://zerodha.com/tos/gtt)). Orders use a registered static IP through the Oracle tunnel, meeting the April 2026 requirement ([Zerodha](https://support.zerodha.com/category/trading-and-markets/general-kite/kite-api/articles/static-ip)), and engine orders are LIMIT. Registration is not needed at or below 10 orders a second ([Groww](https://groww.in/blog/nse-issues-new-compliance-norms-for-retail-algo-trading)).

Four items remain open. The 10-per-second limit holds by design but no limiter enforces it, and a hard limiter is a small fix. Nothing sets market_protection, while a broker-forum reply says MARKET orders with "0" are rejected ([Kite forum](https://kite.trade/forum/discussion/comment/52171)). The audit confines this to manual MARKET orders, but those are the emergency path, so verify it with Zerodha and confirm that the kill switch and fallback route cannot depend on them. The kill switch counts as protection only if it can trade. LIMIT exits can also sit unfilled in a gap, so backtests should fill stops at the next tradable price, including band-locked days, and clamp re-placed limits inside the band, since a limit outside it may be rejected. Finally, the research could not confirm daily-logout mechanics on Kite or DDPI status, and one unverified forum reply says paper trading on live Kite data is restricted ([Kite forum](https://kite.trade/forum/discussion/comment/53060/)). Check these with Zerodha directly.

Add a days-to-exit cap beside the capital-based 8% limit, confirm that the 8% applies to notional, run a -13% no-exit stress based on 23 March 2020 ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/article/news-cm/sensex-nifty-crash-10-hit-lower-circuit-120032300280_1.html)), assume market-wide locks such as the reported 848 stocks on 4 June 2024 ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/news/848-stocks-locked-in-lower-circuit-on-june-4-post-ls-polls-here-what-it-means-124060400745_1.html), headline only), and keep a documented manual exit on a second broker.

## A realistic target: Sharpe 0.8, built on stock skill measured against passive

The rethink is to stop asking how to get from 0.60 to 1.4, and ask whether stock selection adds anything a passive NIFTY and gold mix does not. Today that increment is about 0.12-0.17 of Sharpe, and it is the only thing the engine is really selling. Four changes follow. Make the passive mix the hurdle for every stream and report alpha against NIFTY plus gold. Treat gold as a capped, disclosed allocation, not as evidence of skill. Rebuild the stock book as a slower, lower-turnover, multi-factor ensemble that keeps winners longer. Budget risk across the four separated sources by risk weight, and accept the CAGR that follows instead of chasing it with leverage.

| Tier | Sharpe | Vol | CAGR | Calmar | Requirement |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Today | 0.60 | 11% | 13% | 0.44 | Selection-risk downside 0.39-0.48 |
| **Planning base** | 0.65-0.75 | 13-15% | 14-17% | about 0.5-0.6 | Lower turnover, idle capital deployed |
| **Realistic target** | **about 0.8** | **about 15%** | **17-18%** | **about 0.6-0.7** | Stock core near 0.75, one new stream by risk |
| **Stretch** | 0.9-1.0 | 18-20% | 21-24% | about 0.7 | All of the above live, plus modest index-futures leverage |
| Golden | 1.4 | 22% | 35% | 1.0 | No documented positional precedent |

CAGR is my calculation from the formula above, and the Calmar ranges are judgement, assuming a tail drawdown of about 25-30% persists. Keep PBO ≤ 30% on the metric actually used for selection, and add DSR ≥ 0.95 at a stated central N. Judge three-year rolling figures as the medians they are, and expect individual windows to exceed the golden numbers without meaning anything. After tax, the audit's 13% is about 10.5%, so an 18% target nets roughly 14.5% on the same ratio.

The first work is cheap and needs no new data, and it should run in this order. Split alpha by rank bucket against NIFTY plus gold, to resolve the IC-alpha conflict. Rerun the book with metals replaced by cash, to size the hindsight and the crash dependence. Test longer holds with hold bands as a single pre-registered change. Only then spend trials on factor blends, REIT/InvIT and a short leg. If stock-only alpha turns out near zero, the correct conclusion is that the passive mix plus a risk overlay is the product, and that outcome would be worth knowing before any real capital is ramped.

## Conclusion

The golden target's error is not ambition but category: it borrows Sharpe from high-breadth, cheaply financed short-horizon businesses and CAGR from single bull years, then demands both from a long-biased positional book in one market. The audit's regime arithmetic makes this concrete in a way precedent cannot, because the 25% ceiling comes from Centurion's own NIFTY data rather than from anyone else's failure. Treat a future backtest showing the golden numbers as a result to disprove.

The more surprising finding is how the priorities invert. Crash correlations and tail defence, the usual worries, look healthy, and leverage is settled by Centurion's own experiment. The open questions are nearer home: why alpha and IC disagree, how much of 0.60 is gold bought with hindsight, and whether turnover earns its keep. Those can be answered from existing records within weeks, and they decide whether the realistic target is 0.8 or whether the honest product is closer to a well-run passive mix with a defensive overlay. What remains unknowable from 18 years of data is the true Sharpe to within about ±0.5, which is why the next real evidence must come from small live capital and fill data, not from another backtest.
