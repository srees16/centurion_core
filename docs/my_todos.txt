## Background (for context, not a spec)
A dead man's switch reacts when something stops checking in. There are two kinds:

1. **System liveness.** The app sends periodic heartbeats. If they stop, an external watcher alerts me and may take a protective action.
2. **Operator liveness.** If I haven't acknowledged or checked in within a window during market hours, the system reduces risk on its own (for example, by pausing new entries).

Design principles I care about:
- **External watcher.** The watcher must run outside the process and machine it monitors, because a crashed process or dead host cannot report its own death.
- **Heartbeat = healthy, not alive.** Send a heartbeat only when the main loop is progressing, market data is fresh, and the broker connection and auth are valid. A running but stuck process should miss its heartbeat.
- **Schedule awareness.** Expect heartbeats only during trading hours, accounting for holidays and pre-open. Alert if the system did not start before market open (for example, because a daily broker login or access token expired).
- **Escalation before action.** First an alert, then a louder alert (push or call), then, only after a grace period, an automatic action.
- **Low-regret automatic actions.** Prefer "pause new entries" and "cancel open orders" over "flatten positions." Flattening from an external system during a brief network blip is risky.
- **Broker as source of truth.** After any action, verify open orders and positions against the broker, not internal state.
- **Latched, persisted halt state.** Once halted, the app stays halted across restarts until I manually reset it with a reason. It never auto-resumes.
- **Least-privilege credentials.** If the external watcher can act on the broker, it holds credentials and becomes an attack surface. Give it minimal permissions and handle secrets properly, or consider alert-only.
- **Watch the watcher, minimally.** Use a reputable external service, or have the watcher emit its own periodic heartbeat. No infinite chains.
